2. Was beim Ansehen dieses Berichts passiert

Wird der Bericht als Datei geöffnet, entstehen keine Protokolle.

3. Welche Daten das Programm verarbeitet

Das Programm ruft über die offiziellen Schnittstellen von Apple (App Store Connect API, öffentliche Bewertungsübersicht) und Google (Play Developer API) Daten zu den eigenen Apps des Betreibers ab und speichert sie lokal als Dateien. Alle Aufrufe sind nur lesend.

  • Rezensionen: Sternebewertung, Titel, Text, Datum, Land, Sprache, Store-Kennung der Rezension, bei Google zusätzlich App-Version, Betriebssystem und Gerätemodell, außerdem die Antwort des Entwicklers. Nutzernamen der Rezensenten werden nicht gespeichert und erscheinen nicht im Bericht.
  • Bewertungsübersicht: offizielle Durchschnittsnote und Anzahl je App und Land.
  • Stabilität und Testbetrieb: aggregierte Zählwerte zu Abstürzen, Sitzungen, Builds und Installationen aus Apple Analytics, TestFlight und Google Play. Keine Absturzprotokolle, keine Tester-Namen oder E-Mail-Adressen.

Rezensionen veröffentlichen die Verfasser selbst in den Stores. Rechtsgrundlage der Auswertung ist das berechtigte Interesse des Betreibers, die Qualität seiner Apps zu beobachten und auf Rückmeldungen zu reagieren (Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. f DSGVO). Zugangsdaten zu den Schnittstellen bleiben beim Betreiber und werden weder im Bericht noch in Protokollen ausgegeben.

4. Speicherung, Empfänger, Übermittlung

  • Ort: lokal auf dem Rechner oder Server des Betreibers (Dateien unter data/ und reports/), keine Datenbank, kein Cloud-Dienst des Programms.
  • Dauer: Rezensionsdaten werden fortlaufend ergänzt und nicht automatisch gelöscht, damit Verläufe über längere Zeit auswertbar bleiben. Löschung erfolgt bewusst durch den Betreiber (reset, mit Sicherung).
  • Empfänger: der Betreiber und die Personen, denen er den Bericht oder die Zusammenfassungen bereitstellt (z. B. der jeweilige App-Kunde). Eine Weitergabe an sonstige Dritte findet nicht statt.
  • Drittland: Beim Abruf kommuniziert das Programm mit Apple Inc. (USA) und Google LLC (USA) über deren Schnittstellen. Hierfür gelten die Datenschutzbestimmungen der Anbieter und deren Standardvertragsklauseln beziehungsweise das EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Diese Funktion ist standardmäßig aus und wird nur aktiv, wenn der Betreiber in der Konfiguration einen Anbieter freischaltet und einen eigenen Zugangsschlüssel hinterlegt. Es ist immer genau ein Anbieter aktiv.

  • Mögliche Empfänger: OpenAI (USA), Google LLC (Gemini, USA), Anthropic PBC (Claude, USA), Perplexity AI (USA) oder xAI (Grok, USA), je nach Auswahl des Betreibers. Der Bericht nennt bei jedem KI-Text Anbieter und Modell.
  • Lokale Verarbeitung (Anbieter „local“, z. B. Ollama oder LM Studio): Wählt der Betreiber den lokalen Anbieter, läuft das Sprachmodell auf seinem eigenen Rechner; die Rezensionen werden nur an diesen Rechner selbst (127.0.0.1) übergeben, es gibt keinen externen Empfänger und keine Übermittlung in ein Drittland. Die übrigen Angaben dieses Abschnitts gelten entsprechend.

Rezensionen werden nummeriert übergeben; die Store-Kennung der Rezension, Nutzernamen (werden ohnehin nicht gespeichert), Zugangsdaten und Store-Kennzahlen verlassen den Rechner nicht. Die Maßnahmenvorschläge beruhen auf einer Anfrage ohne Rezensionstexte; sie enthält nur Zählungen und die von der KI zuvor formulierten Kernaussagen.

Privacy policy

For this report and the app-ratings-reviews tool. Last updated: October 2026.

1. Controller

Christian Drapatz, OneTwoApps, Kämpenstraße 42, 45147 Essen, Germany. E-mail: support@onetwoapps.com. No data protection officer has been appointed.

2. What happens when you view this report

  • No data transfer. The report is a finished HTML file. It loads no fonts, scripts or images from third-party servers and sends nothing to third parties. There is no visitor counter and no analytics tool.
  • No cookies. The report sets no cookies. It only remembers display settings in your browser: language, light/dark mode and theme colour (localStorage, until browser data is cleared) and the table sort order (sessionStorage, until the tab is closed). These values never leave the browser.
  • No user accounts, no profiles, no sharing with advertising networks.
  • Hosting. If the report is accessed via an internal web server of the operator, the usual technical access logs arise there (IP address, time, requested file, browser). The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR (operation and security); the logs are deleted according to the settings of the respective server. If the report is opened as a file, no logs are created.

3. What data the tool processes

The tool retrieves data about the operator’s own apps through the official interfaces of Apple (App Store Connect API, public rating summary) and Google (Play Developer API) and stores it locally as files. All calls are read-only.

  • Reviews: star rating, title, text, date, country, language, the store’s review identifier, for Google additionally app version, operating system and device model, plus the developer’s reply. Reviewer user names are not stored and do not appear in the report.
  • Rating summary: official average rating and count per app and country.
  • Stability and testing: aggregated counts of crashes, sessions, builds and installs from Apple Analytics, TestFlight and Google Play. No crash logs, no tester names or e-mail addresses.

Reviews are published by their authors in the stores themselves. The legal basis for the analysis is the operator’s legitimate interest in monitoring the quality of its apps and responding to feedback (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). Credentials for the interfaces remain with the operator and are never written to the report or to logs.

4. Storage, recipients, transfers

  • Location: locally on the operator’s computer or server (files under data/ and reports/), no database, no cloud service of the tool.
  • Retention: review data is added to continuously and not deleted automatically so that trends remain analysable over time. Deletion is a deliberate action by the operator (reset, with backup).
  • Recipients: the operator and the people to whom it provides the report or the summaries (e.g. the respective app customer). There is no disclosure to other third parties.
  • Third countries: when fetching, the tool communicates with Apple Inc. (USA) and Google LLC (USA) through their interfaces. The providers’ privacy policies and their standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework apply.

4a. AI analysis (optional, only if the operator enables it)

The operator can additionally have reviews analysed by a language model of an AI provider (summary per app, sentiment per review, reply drafts, topic suggestions, suggested measures, questions to the data). This function is off by default and becomes active only when the operator enables a provider in the configuration and supplies their own access key. Exactly one provider is active at a time.

  • Possible recipients: OpenAI (USA), Google LLC (Gemini, USA), Anthropic PBC (Claude, USA), Perplexity AI (USA) or xAI (Grok, USA), depending on the operator’s choice. The report names provider and model next to every AI text.
  • Local processing (provider “local”, e.g. Ollama or LM Studio): if the operator chooses the local provider, the language model runs on the operator’s own computer; the reviews are passed only to that computer itself (127.0.0.1), there is no external recipient and no transfer to a third country. The remaining statements of this section apply accordingly.
  • Data transmitted: title and text of the review (can be switched off via sendReviewText, then only figures are sent), star rating, date, app version, language, country, app name, whether a developer reply exists. Reviews are passed with running numbers; the store’s review identifier, user names (not stored anyway), credentials and store figures never leave the computer. The suggested measures come from a request without review texts; it contains only counts and the core statements the AI itself phrased earlier.
  • Competitors: if the operator has listed third-party Apple apps as competitors, their public reviews (from Apple’s public feed, same fields per review) are sent to the provider as well, unless analyzeCompetitors is switched off; no reply drafts are produced for them.
  • Scope: only new or changed reviews; an app’s summary is based on at most the most recent reviews (setting summaryReviews), a question on at most maxReviewsPerQuestion reviews. A monthly limit caps the volume.
  • Legal basis: the operator’s legitimate interest in analysing publicly posted reviews (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). Transfers to the USA are covered by the chosen provider’s standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and its terms on the use of inputs; the operator chooses a provider whose API terms exclude using inputs for training.
  • Questions in the report: if the operator asks questions directly in the report, a service runs solely on the operator’s own computer (127.0.0.1); the question goes to the same provider as above, no further recipients arise. The report published via URL does not contain this function.
  • Result: the AI texts are stored locally in data/ and marked “AI” in the report. They are suggestions; reply drafts are never sent to the store automatically. Quotes are checked against the original text.

4b. Notification (optional, only if the operator enables it)

After the daily fetch the operator can be notified when new reviews with one or two stars have arrived, an app’s rating status (traffic light) has changed or the crash rate has risen. This function is off by default.

  • Recipients: solely the e-mail addresses entered by the operator via their own mail server, their Slack workspace (Slack Technologies, USA) or a webhook service they run or commission themselves. The tool itself runs no service.
  • Data transmitted: per reported review the star rating, date, country, app version, title and text (shortened to 300 characters), the app name, the reasons of the traffic light and, if the AI analysis is active, its stored one-line summary of the review. No user names (not stored), no store review identifier, no credentials, no store figures. No new request to an AI provider is made for the notification.
  • Transfer: e-mail only encrypted (STARTTLS or SSL), webhooks only via HTTPS. Credentials live solely in the operator’s environment variables.
  • Legal basis: the operator’s legitimate interest in reacting promptly to publicly posted criticism of their apps (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). For Slack its privacy policy and standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework apply.
